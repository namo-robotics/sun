That's your three passes with one difference: specializations are discovered on demand, not by a scan. A syntactic scan can only find specializations that appear in annotations (Vec<u8> in a signature or field). Many arise only during body analysis: Vec<Owner>(alloc, 4) in an expression, Option.Some(x) with an inferred type argument, generic function calls, and specializations nested inside other specializations' bodies. So instantiation has to stay lazy anyway, and a dedicated "find all specializations" pass would be incomplete by construction.

The invariant that actually matters — and that A2 establishes — is "before any body is analyzed, every declaration's type and shape is registered". Once that holds, when a specialization is created stops mattering: its body can reference anything.

The one residual gap is the mirror of what bit us: if a class shape itself (a field type or method param) instantiates a generic during A2, that specialization's body is analyzed right then, and could reference a class whose shape comes later in A2. Nothing in the stdlib or the proto generator does that, so I haven't addressed it. If it ever bites, the clean completion of your idea is: instantiation registers the specialization's shape immediately (so callers type-check) and its bodies are analyzed after all shapes are known — i.e., "specializations declared in pass 2, bodies in pass 3", exactly as you describe. That's a small, contained change to instantiateGenericClass (defer PASS 2 into a queue flushed at the end of collectDeclarations), and I'd suggest doing it as its own PR rather than inside this feature.
